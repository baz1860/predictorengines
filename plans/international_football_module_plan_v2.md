# International Football Module Plan — v2

**Date:** 8 August 2026

**Status:** Draft for approval

**Audience:** Decision-maker, product owner and implementing engineer

**Reworks:** `plans/international_football_module_plan.md`

**Retains technical compatibility requirements from:** `plans/international-refactor-plan.md`

---

## 1. Executive decision

We should expand the World Cup engine into an **International Football** engine, but we should
not approve a worldwide betting product, a fixed twelve-week delivery promise, or a recurring data
budget yet.

The existing code gives us a useful head start: it can calculate baseline probabilities for many
national teams from a large international-results history. It is not, however, an operational
international-football system. Its fixture ingestion, odds capture, squad data, tournament
simulation, reporting, validation and application identity remain substantially World Cup-specific.

The programme will therefore deliver four products in sequence:

| Release | Product | Default betting policy |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | Worldwide senior men's **competitive fixture discovery and predictions** | No automated betting |
| **R2** | Senior men's **friendly predictions**, clearly separated in reporting | Friendlies blocked from betting |
| **R3** | Odds and bet candidates for supported **competitive** competitions | Fail closed unless all evidence gates pass |
| **R4** | Possible friendly betting | No committed date; earned only through forward evidence |

The first commitment is a bounded discovery and architecture phase. The full build is approved only
after fixture, odds and lineup coverage have been measured through one complete high-volume
international window.

### Recommended decision now

Approve:

1. the scope and data-contract work in Phase 0;
2. the provider evidence work in Phase 1;
3. the compatibility safety harness needed to make later work reversible.

Do not yet approve:

- a claim of complete worldwide fixture coverage;
- a £0 or £30 monthly operating assumption;
- automated betting outside the World Cup;
- a delivery date for friendly betting;
- renaming the production engine before the shadow system passes its gates.

## 2. Product scope

### 2.1 Core match universe

For v1 of the International Football engine, **international football** means:

- senior men's national teams;
- current FIFA members in all six confederations;
- competitive matches and friendlies in which both participants are in the canonical team registry;
- pre-match prediction only.

The canonical registry may include confederation associate members, but they receive the worldwide
coverage promise only after Phase 1 demonstrates provider coverage for them.

This definition replaces the ambiguous phrases “every national team” and “teams active in the last
four years.” A current FIFA member does not disappear from scope merely because it has been dormant.
Sparse or dormant teams remain in scope but receive an explicit weak-evidence status.

### 2.2 Expansion scope

The historical dataset also contains former countries, non-FIFA teams, regional selections and
representative sides. They remain usable for historical rating continuity where appropriate, but
they are not part of the operational coverage service-level objective unless individually approved
in the registry.

Examples that require explicit policy rather than automatic inclusion include successor states,
CONIFA teams, island selections and regional representative teams.

### 2.3 Explicitly out of scope

- women's international football;
- youth, Olympic, B-team and age-group football;
- club football;
- in-play betting;
- player props;
- universal tournament simulation for all competitions;
- scraping 211 association websites as a production dependency;
- claims of profitable betting before forward, decision-time evidence exists.

### 2.4 Product terminology

The system must use these terms consistently:

- **Prediction:** model probabilities for a fixture.
- **Price:** prediction compared with current bookmaker odds.
- **Bet candidate:** a price that passes model, data, quote and portfolio gates.
- **Competition profile:** rules needed to simulate a particular tournament.
- **Coverage:** observed data completeness, not a synonym for model confidence.
- **Model support:** how much relevant historical evidence exists for the teams and context.

## 3. Repository baseline — what exists today

The following is the measured baseline on 8 August 2026. These numbers are evidence, not marketing
claims, and should be regenerated by Phase 0.

### 3.1 Results and identities

| Measure | Current value |
|---|---:|
| Rows in `data/results.csv` | 49,529 |
| Rows with two scores | 49,523 |
| Scoreless rows currently classified as upcoming | 6 |
| Unique team identities | 338 |
| Competition labels | 201 |
| Friendly rows | 18,388 |
| Teams appearing since 1 January 2023 | 256 |
| Exact duplicate match keys found | 1 |

The six scoreless rows are dated 6–19 July 2026 and are already in the past. They demonstrate that
“blank score means upcoming” is not a safe fixture lifecycle.

### 3.2 Model and application

What is genuinely reusable:

- chronological Elo updates over the international-results history;
- an Elo-to-goals Poisson model;
- a Dixon-Coles attack/defence model with time decay;
- scoreline and 1X2 probability generation;
- World Cup simulation logic that can be preserved as one profile;
- edge, bankroll and settlement components that can be adapted;
- an adapter-based application surface;
- club-soccer precedents for provider reconciliation, identity review and evidence reporting.

What must not be misrepresented:

- only 12 competition labels have explicit Elo importance weights; the other 189 use a generic
  default, affecting 34% of played history;
- the context adjustment excludes friendlies and is opt-in;
- the confederation adjustment was calibrated on World Cups;
- squad and lineup data are World Cup-specific;
- the current market-blend artifact is inactive and assigns the model zero weight;
- the ordinary application prediction path does not consume a competition or the optional context,
  squad and market adjustments;
- the registry has no legacy-alias mechanism today;
- the daily validation failure is logged but does not stop the pipeline.

### 3.3 Correct conclusion

The existing system is a strong **baseline model and World Cup product**. It is not already a
worldwide operational product. The expansion is a controlled data-platform, compatibility and model
evaluation project—not the removal of two filters.

## 4. Delivery principles

1. **Separate observations, fixtures and results.** Mutable schedules must not share a table with
   historical outcomes.
2. **Provider claims are hypotheses until measured.** A competition name in a catalogue is not
   evidence of completeness, timeliness or field quality.
3. **Fail closed for betting.** Missing identity, neutral status, quote provenance or model support
   can still permit display, but cannot permit a bet candidate.
4. **Use stable internal identities.** Human-readable names are attributes, not primary keys.
5. **Preserve point-in-time truth.** Training and validation may use only information available at
   the forecast timestamp.
6. **Do not rename production early.** Build and shadow the International engine beside the legacy
   path; cut over last.
7. **Separate refactor equivalence from model improvement.** A refactor must reproduce frozen
   outputs. A model change must pass an explicit challenger gate.
8. **Make calendar time visible.** Some evidence can only be collected during international windows;
   engineering effort and elapsed time are different schedules.
9. **No silent fallback for unknown competitions or teams.** Unknowns are quarantined and surfaced.
10. **Every phase is reversible.** The World Cup engine remains usable until final cutover.

## 5. Target architecture

### 5.1 Package layout

Create `international/` as the eventual source of truth:

```text
international/
  model/                 # Elo, DC, calibration, context and support assessment
  competitions/          # canonical competition registry and simulation profiles
  providers/             # provider clients, caches and normalizers
  identities/            # teams, aliases, adjudication and provider mappings
  markets/               # odds normalization, pricing, eligibility and settlement
  data/                   # canonical runtime artifacts, not raw secrets
  cli/                    # preferred commands; legacy wrappers delegate here
  paths.py                # all canonical and compatibility paths
```

Do not move production files during the first refactor. Introduce paths and delegation first, prove
equivalence, and migrate data only after the new code is stable.

### 5.2 Data layers

#### A. Raw provider observations — append-only

Store each response before normalization with:

- provider;
- endpoint and request parameters, excluding secrets;
- retrieval timestamp;
- response hash;
- schema/version metadata;
- success, empty-success or error status.

Raw observations make normalization replayable and provider changes auditable.

#### B. Canonical fixtures — mutable latest state plus history

Minimum fields:

| Group | Required fields |
|---|---|
| Identity | `fixture_id`, provider event IDs, canonical home/away team IDs, competition ID |
| Time | `kickoff_utc`, venue timezone, local calendar date |
| Context | venue ID/name/city/country, neutral status and provenance |
| Lifecycle | scheduled, postponed, cancelled, abandoned, live, finished, unknown |
| Competition | canonical type, confederation, stage/round/group where applicable |
| Provenance | winning source, all contributing sources, first/last observed timestamps |
| Quality | identity, kickoff, venue, neutral and status quality flags |

Maintain fixture-state history so a cancellation or reschedule is represented as a transition, not
as deletion or a second unrelated fixture.

#### C. Historical results

`results.csv` remains a historical model input during transition. A finished canonical fixture is
promoted or reconciled into results only when both teams, score, competition and status are valid.
Upstream historical refreshes remain independently reproducible.

#### D. Market observations — append-only

Each quote must retain:

- fixture ID;
- provider and bookmaker;
- market, side and line;
- decimal odds;
- provider update time and retrieval time;
- pre-match/in-play status;
- region and request provenance.

Opening, decision-time and closing prices are derived views; they must not overwrite observations.

### 5.3 Fixture identity

Do not key fixtures solely by date and team names. Use an internal immutable ID and retain provider
IDs. Reconciliation may consider teams, competition, kickoff proximity and venue, but ambiguous
matches go to quarantine.

A kickoff move across midnight must update one fixture, not create a duplicate. A postponed match
must not remain priceable. A same-day rematch must remain representable.

### 5.4 Team identity

Create a versioned team registry containing:

- immutable team ID;
- display and canonical names;
- FIFA/confederation identifiers and membership status;
- senior-men eligibility;
- predecessor/successor links where relevant;
- provider-scoped aliases;
- alias validity dates where names change;
- manual adjudication record and rationale.

Fuzzy matching may suggest candidates for human review. It must never merge identities in production.

### 5.5 Competition identity

Create a versioned competition registry. Each entry declares:

- immutable competition ID and canonical label;
- friendly/qualifier/finals/regional classification;
- confederation and senior-men eligibility;
- existing Elo weight, preserved initially;
- provider identifiers and aliases;
- whether a simulation profile exists;
- market and betting policy;
- unknown-name behavior.

Phase 1 must map every currently observed historical label. A change to importance weights is a model
challenger, not part of the refactor.

### 5.6 Tournament simulation

World Cup 2026 becomes a preserved profile. Other profiles are added only when there is a product
need for tournament probabilities.

A profile owns:

- participants and fixtures;
- group/table rules and tie-breaks;
- advancement and playoff routes;
- bracket construction;
- stage-specific output columns;
- a rules source and effective date.

Ordinary friendlies and qualifiers need fixture probabilities; they do not need fake tournament
profiles.

## 6. Provider evidence plan

### 6.1 What Phase 1 must determine

Test BSD, API-Football, TheSportsDB, openfootball and any other serious candidate against the same
fixture sample. The evaluation is by competition and confederation, not by a few prominent matches.

For each provider, record:

- future horizon;
- fixture recall and false-positive rate;
- announcement and change latency;
- cancellations and postponements;
- stable event and team IDs;
- kickoff UTC and timezone correctness;
- competition, venue and neutral-ground fields;
- lineup, squad and unavailable-player availability and publication time;
- odds markets, bookmaker counts and quote timestamps;
- pagination, quotas and credit calculation;
- raw-response retention rights, licence and production terms;
- outage behavior and schema stability.

### 6.2 Evidence periods

Phase 1 has two parts:

1. **Five-day technical spike:** authenticate, enumerate catalogues, save sample responses and test
   normalizers.
2. **One complete high-volume international window:** run all candidate sources in shadow mode and
   compare them with at least one independent reference.

The second part is calendar-bound. It cannot be replaced by extrapolating from a World Cup feed or
from the existence of an “International Friendlies” catalogue entry.

### 6.3 Fixture provider gate

For the core scope over the observed window:

- fixture recall at least **99.0%** against the adjudicated reference set;
- fixture precision at least **99.5%**;
- at least **99.0%** of scheduled fixtures with valid kickoff UTC, teams and competition;
- neutral status known or independently derivable for at least **98.0%**;
- 95% of observed schedule changes reflected within **six hours**;
- no cancelled/postponed fixture left priceable after its status is known;
- all unknown identities quarantined and visible;
- normalized output reproducible from cached raw responses.

If no provider stack meets this gate, change the public coverage promise. Manual entry may cover a
curated subset, but it is not an acceptable fallback for a worldwide SLA.

### 6.4 Odds provider gate

The Odds API currently exposes selected international competitions, not a universal friendlies and
qualifying feed. Its quota cost is based on markets × regions, not simply HTTP calls. Therefore the
spike must produce an explicit monthly budget model:

```text
credits = competition_keys × polls × markets × regions
```

For each target competition, measure:

- percentage of fixtures with a complete 1X2 market;
- median and minimum bookmaker count;
- first price availability and last pre-kickoff update;
- totals and BTTS availability;
- provider/team identity join rate;
- stale, suspended and in-play quote rejection;
- projected credits and cost under the proposed polling schedule.

R3 proceeds competition by competition. A paid plan is not treated as universal coverage unless the
catalogue evidence says so.

### 6.5 Squad and lineup evidence

Distinguish four different data products:

1. announced squad;
2. unavailable players;
3. projected lineup;
4. confirmed lineup.

Do not describe confirmed lineups as a substitute for announced squads. Record publication time so
every feature can be evaluated at the decision timestamp.

No squad feature may enter a historical backtest unless equivalent point-in-time historical data
exists. New captures build a future research asset; they do not retroactively justify a model.

### 6.6 Phase 1 deliverable

Write `docs/international/provider_evidence_YYYY-MM-DD.md` containing:

- source catalogue and terms;
- raw-response hashes and redacted examples;
- coverage matrices;
- conflict and latency statistics;
- quota/cost calculation;
- selected primary and fallback sources per field;
- unsupported competitions;
- go/no-go recommendation and revised product promise.

## 7. Prediction and modelling plan

### 7.1 Preserve a baseline before improving anything

Freeze:

- fixed-input World Cup match probabilities;
- fixed-seed simulation output;
- CLI schemas and exit behavior;
- app adapter output;
- edge-template and report schemas;
- settlement of representative legacy ledger rows;
- hashes of relevant fitted artifacts and input data.

The first International path must reproduce these outputs on the same inputs. This proves the
refactor, not the quality of any new model.

### 7.2 Competition taxonomy before new weights

Map all 201 historical labels into reviewed categories while preserving current Elo weights. Report
every label that would change effective weight under a proposed taxonomy.

A weight change requires:

- a written hypothesis;
- walk-forward fitting;
- comparison with the frozen incumbent;
- performance by competition type and confederation;
- a promoted, versioned artifact only if gates pass.

### 7.3 Baseline evaluation by match type

Before adding friendly features, score the incumbent using temporal walk-forward predictions for:

- tournament finals;
- qualifiers;
- Nations Leagues;
- regional competitions;
- friendlies;
- neutral and non-neutral matches;
- evidence tiers and confederation pairings.

Report:

- sample size;
- 1X2 Brier score;
- multiclass log loss;
- accuracy for readability, not as the promotion metric;
- expected calibration error and reliability tables;
- bootstrap confidence intervals;
- skipped and unsupported matches.

### 7.4 Friendly prediction policy

R2 initially uses the validated baseline and labels the match as a friendly. It does not claim that
a new “motivation” model exists.

Potential challengers include:

- competition-specific intercept or home advantage;
- probability flattening fitted from historical residuals;
- goal overdispersion if supported by held-out scoreline likelihood;
- decision-time squad strength where historical evidence exists;
- team-specific friendly behavior only where shrinkage and samples support it;
- days to/from a competitive match using the known calendar.

Every challenger must use information available at forecast time. “Favourites probably
underperform” is a hypothesis to test, not an implementation instruction.

### 7.5 Model promotion gate

A challenger is promoted only if:

- temporal train, validation and test periods do not overlap;
- preprocessing and hyperparameters are fitted without test data;
- global log loss and Brier do not regress beyond the agreed tolerance;
- no major competition bucket with at least 200 test matches regresses by more than 0.01 Brier;
- the target bucket shows a practically useful improvement with bootstrap uncertainty reported;
- calibration does not materially worsen;
- artifacts contain input hashes, code version, fit period and promotion decision.

Failure to promote is an acceptable research result. R2 can ship baseline friendly predictions with
betting disabled.

### 7.6 Model support and data quality

Do not compress every uncertainty into a single “confidence” label. Each prediction carries:

- `model_support`: supported, thin or unsupported;
- `fixture_quality`: complete, partial or conflicted;
- `squad_evidence`: announced, availability-only, lineup-only or absent;
- `market_quality`: complete, thin, stale or absent;
- `bet_eligible`: true/false with machine-readable reasons.

Initial model-support rules must consider recent match count, last match date, whether the team is in
the fitted model and parameter shrinkage. Thresholds are versioned and validated; they are not copied
blindly from club football.

## 8. Betting policy

### 8.1 Default policy

- R1: no automated betting.
- R2: all friendlies blocked.
- R3: competitive fixtures only, and only supported competitions.
- Unknown, thin, conflicted or stale inputs fail closed.

### 8.2 Eligibility gate per candidate

A bet candidate requires:

- supported teams and competition;
- valid fixture lifecycle and future kickoff;
- known neutral/home context;
- fresh complete market with quote timestamp;
- minimum bookmaker evidence or an approved bookmaker source;
- validated model version for that competition type;
- no open identity or source conflict;
- portfolio and exposure checks;
- auditable prediction and quote snapshots.

### 8.3 Forward evidence gate

Do not infer profitability from backfilled closing prices or current-state lineups. Betting promotion
requires locked, pre-kickoff decisions accumulated prospectively.

For a competition class, report at minimum:

- number of independent fixtures and candidates;
- price and stake available at decision time;
- closing-line value where a valid close exists;
- yield and drawdown with confidence intervals;
- results by market and odds band;
- quote rejection and missing-close rates.

Friendly betting remains disabled until at least 500 prospectively captured, independently priced
friendly decisions or two complete international seasons—whichever is later—and the pre-registered
promotion test passes. This is a calendar-bound evidence requirement, not a development estimate.

## 9. Application and backward compatibility

### 9.1 Engine identities

During shadow operation:

- keep `worldcup` as the production engine;
- register `international_shadow` only in development/shadow environments;
- compare outputs without changing ledgers or user workflows.

At cutover:

- primary engine ID becomes `international`;
- `worldcup` becomes a tested alias resolving to the same adapter with default competition
  `worldcup_2026`;
- `/api/engines` lists only the primary engine;
- legacy API lookups continue working;
- duplicate ID and alias conflicts fail at startup.

### 9.2 CLI compatibility

Preferred commands:

```text
python3 -m international.cli.predict ...
python3 -m international.cli.fixtures ...
python3 -m international.cli.edge --competition <id>
python3 -m international.cli.simulate --competition worldcup_2026
python3 -m international.cli.validate ...
```

Legacy root commands remain wrappers for at least one release cycle. Existing imports used by tests
continue to re-export their public functions.

### 9.3 Output compatibility

For World Cup 2026, continue to mirror:

- `predictions_worldcup_2026.csv`;
- `tournament_odds.csv`;
- `edge_report.csv`;
- `bet_queue.csv`;
- `data/worldcup/card.md`.

New canonical outputs are competition-aware and include competition and fixture IDs. Mirrors are
removed only after downstream dashboard, report and user workflows have migrated.

### 9.4 Ledger and settlement

- Existing `engine=worldcup` rows remain settleable.
- New rows use `engine=international`, canonical fixture ID and competition ID.
- Settlement must not rely only on date and display names.
- Migration is additive; never rewrite settled economic records unnecessarily.
- Dry-run settlement compares old and new resolvers before cutover.

### 9.5 User interface

Add competition selection to predict and edge schemas. Add explicit simulation-profile selection to
the simulation UI; the current generic simulation form does not render arbitrary filters.

The fixtures page must show status, kickoff timezone, source freshness and quality conflicts. The
edge page must show betting-ineligibility reasons instead of merely hiding candidates.

## 10. Implementation phases

Durations below are engineering effort for one engineer. Calendar-bound observation is shown
separately and cannot be compressed by parallel coding.

### Phase 0 — Scope, baseline and contracts

**Engineering effort:** 1 week

Work:

- approve the core match universe and expansion policy;
- regenerate the repository baseline in Section 3;
- freeze golden CLI, app, simulation, edge and settlement behavior;
- define canonical fixture, team, competition and market schemas;
- define product metrics, operational SLOs and betting policy;
- create a decision log for unresolved scope questions.

Exit criteria:

- signed scope and data contracts;
- automated legacy goldens passing;
- no undefined use of “all,” “active,” “confidence” or “international team”;
- a reproducible baseline report committed.

### Phase 1 — Provider evidence

**Engineering effort:** 1 week for the spike

**Elapsed evidence time:** one complete international window

Work:

- call and cache every candidate source;
- build temporary normalizers and comparison tooling;
- measure coverage, conflicts, timeliness, fields, quotas and terms;
- calculate actual odds polling cost;
- observe one full window in shadow mode.

Exit criteria:

- provider evidence document complete;
- selected source stack passes Section 6 gates, or product scope is reduced;
- unsupported competitions explicitly listed;
- operating-cost range based on measured polling, not headline plan prices.

Stop condition:

If no feasible source stack reaches the core fixture gate, stop the worldwide build and propose a
curated competition product instead.

### Phase 2 — Data foundation

**Engineering effort:** 2–3 weeks

Work:

- implement raw observation storage;
- implement team and competition registries;
- implement fixture normalization, reconciliation, state transitions and quarantine;
- create separate fixture and result stores;
- add provenance, freshness and replay tooling;
- test cancellations, reschedules, midnight moves and provider conflicts.

Exit criteria:

- cached observations reproduce canonical fixtures offline;
- destructive fixture shrink and duplicate guards pass;
- no past cancelled/postponed fixture remains priceable;
- unknown identities and competitions are quarantined;
- results history remains unchanged except through an explicit reconciliation command.

### Phase 3 — Compatibility refactor

**Engineering effort:** 2–3 weeks

Work:

- introduce `international/` and centralized paths;
- copy/delegate World Cup model and market code without behavior changes;
- preserve root wrappers and outputs;
- add registry alias support and tests;
- preserve World Cup simulator as a profile;
- extend ledger metadata without breaking old rows.

Exit criteria:

- frozen World Cup goldens pass on old and new paths;
- app adapter contract tests pass;
- legacy ledger dry-run settlements match;
- no production registration or rename yet.

### Phase 4 — International fixture shadow system

**Engineering effort:** 2–3 weeks

**Elapsed evidence time:** may overlap the Phase 1 observation window where safe

Work:

- run scheduled multi-provider ingestion;
- reconcile and publish the next 90 days of in-scope fixtures;
- produce operational coverage reports;
- test correction, cancellation and provider-outage recovery;
- create prediction snapshots without exposing or betting them in production.

Exit criteria:

- Section 6 fixture gates pass over the adjudicated observation period;
- two consecutive scheduled runs are reproducible and healthy;
- no unresolved high-severity identity or status conflicts;
- on-call/runbook and recovery commands are documented.

### Phase 5 — R1 competitive prediction release

**Engineering effort:** 1–2 weeks

Work:

- expose supported competitive competitions in the shadow adapter;
- add model-support and fixture-quality fields;
- implement competition-level validation reporting;
- add fixtures and competition controls to the application;
- perform a no-bet production canary.

Exit criteria:

- every displayed prediction has canonical fixture and model-version provenance;
- competitive validation and legacy non-regression gates pass;
- unsupported/thin matches are visible and never silently defaulted;
- no automated bets can be recorded from the new path;
- product owner approves R1 wording based on measured coverage.

### Phase 6 — R2 friendly prediction audit and release

**Engineering effort:** 1–2 weeks for baseline audit; challenger work estimated separately

Work:

- publish incumbent friendly metrics and calibration;
- expose friendlies with explicit classification and no-bet policy;
- test only challengers supported by point-in-time historical features;
- begin prospective squad, lineup and prediction capture.

Exit criteria:

- friendly metrics, samples and uncertainty published;
- betting block covered by CLI, app and ledger tests;
- any promoted challenger passes Section 7 gates;
- absence of a promoted challenger does not block baseline R2.

### Phase 7 — R3 competitive odds and betting

**Engineering effort:** 2 weeks per proven source pattern, subject to provider coverage

Work:

- ingest append-only market observations;
- enforce quote freshness and pre-match status;
- snapshot decision-time predictions and quotes;
- implement competition-level eligibility policies and request budgets;
- run preview-only before enabling recording.

Exit criteria:

- odds coverage and quota budget match Phase 1 evidence;
- stale, incomplete and in-play quotes fail closed;
- every candidate is replayable from locked snapshots;
- existing portfolio and settlement tests pass;
- recording enabled only for individually approved competitions.

### Phase 8 — Production rename and cutover

**Engineering effort:** 1 week

Work:

- make `international` primary and `worldcup` an alias;
- migrate dashboard, reporting, provenance and operational scripts;
- keep legacy output mirrors;
- canary, monitor and rehearse rollback.

Exit criteria:

- all legacy API, CLI, ledger and output tests pass;
- production validation is actually blocking;
- cutover health checks pass for two scheduled cycles;
- rollback to the previous adapter is documented and rehearsed.

### Phase 9 — R4 friendly betting research

**Engineering effort:** not estimated until prospective evidence exists

**Elapsed evidence time:** at least two international seasons or the sample gate in Section 8

There is no promised ship date. The deliverable is a go/no-go evidence report.

## 11. Schedule and budget

### 11.1 Engineering estimate

Before optional friendly challengers and R4 research:

- **10–15 engineer-weeks** for Phases 0–5 and production-quality data foundations;
- **2+ engineer-weeks** for competitive odds once provider coverage is proven;
- **1 engineer-week** for final cutover;
- contingency required if no provider meets the worldwide fixture gate.

This is not a promise of 13–18 calendar weeks. At least one full international window must be
observed, and the next suitable window controls elapsed time.

### 11.2 Minimum viable release

The MVP is **R1: supported competitive international predictions with no automated betting**. It
ships only after the shadow fixture gate passes. It does not include a production rename merely to
meet a week number.

### 11.3 Data budget

No recurring spend is approved in advance. Phase 1 returns:

- free-tier feasibility by source;
- monthly credits under the actual polling plan;
- paid alternatives and coverage differences;
- operational cost of manual adjudication;
- a maximum monthly budget proposal.

Free data without adequate coverage, terms, provenance or reliability is not considered zero-cost.

## 12. Validation and release gates

### 12.1 Test layers

1. **Unit:** normalizers, identity rules, lifecycle transitions, competition mappings and market
   parsing.
2. **Contract:** provider response fixtures, cached replay, registry aliases and adapter schemas.
3. **Golden:** fixed World Cup predictions, seeded simulation, output columns and legacy CLI.
4. **Integration:** raw observation → canonical fixture → prediction → quote → candidate → ledger →
   settlement.
5. **Walk-forward:** point-in-time metrics by competition and evidence tier.
6. **Operational:** freshness, quotas, provider outage, cancellation, correction and rollback.

### 12.2 Blocking behavior

The release command and CI must stop on:

- failed model non-regression gate;
- fixture shrink or duplicate corruption;
- unresolved high-severity identity conflicts;
- stale or missing required data for a betting run;
- incompatible ledger migration;
- broken legacy aliases or outputs.

A message followed by successful completion is not a gate.

### 12.3 Success criteria

The programme succeeds when:

1. the in-scope team and match universe is explicit and versioned;
2. canonical fixtures meet the Section 6 coverage gates through a full window;
3. cancellations, postponements and time changes are represented correctly;
4. predictions are reproducible and reported by competition type;
5. every prediction reports separate model-support and data-quality dimensions;
6. World Cup behavior is equivalent on frozen inputs through old and new paths;
7. old `worldcup` ledger rows still settle after cutover;
8. new records contain fixture and competition IDs;
9. validation failures block release and betting;
10. friendlies cannot be bet until the prospective evidence gate passes;
11. cost and provider claims are supported by measured evidence;
12. rollback has been rehearsed successfully.

## 13. Operations and ownership

### 13.1 Scheduled run

A normal run should:

1. collect and retain raw observations;
2. normalize and reconcile fixtures;
3. report conflicts and quarantine;
4. reconcile finished results;
5. refresh or validate model artifacts only when inputs changed;
6. generate predictions and immutable snapshots;
7. collect eligible odds within the request budget;
8. generate preview candidates;
9. run blocking health and validation checks;
10. publish cards and reports only after gates pass.

### 13.2 Observability

Track at minimum:

- provider success, empty-success and error counts;
- last successful retrieval by field and competition;
- fixtures added, changed, cancelled, quarantined and reconciled;
- source conflicts and manual adjudication backlog;
- upcoming fixtures by competition and horizon;
- past scoreless or priceable fixtures;
- odds credits used and projected;
- quote freshness and bookmaker counts;
- model artifact age and input hashes;
- prediction and betting eligibility counts by reason.

### 13.3 Runbook and recovery

Document:

- provider outage and fallback;
- schema change;
- bad merge or unexpected shrink;
- mistaken identity merge;
- postponed/cancelled fixture correction;
- stale odds or quota exhaustion;
- model gate failure;
- ledger settlement discrepancy;
- rollback to the World Cup adapter.

## 14. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation / decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| No provider stack meets worldwide fixture SLO | High | High | Phase 1 hard gate; reduce public scope rather than disguise gaps with manual work |
| Odds catalogue excludes friendlies or confederations | High | High for betting | Competition-by-competition R3; paid plans do not imply universal coverage |
| Schedule changes create duplicate or zombie fixtures | High | High | Stable IDs, lifecycle history, tombstones and past-scoreless health gate |
| Team identity merges corrupt ratings or settlement | Medium | High | Provider IDs, scoped aliases, quarantine and human adjudication |
| Neutral status is missing or wrong | Medium | High | Field-level provenance; no betting without approved context |
| Friendly challenger cannot be trained historically | High | Medium | Ship baseline no-bet predictions; build prospective dataset; no invented feature |
| Pooled validation hides a competition regression | High | High | Metrics and gates by competition and support tier |
| Refactor breaks World Cup or old ledgers | Medium | High | Copy/delegate, frozen goldens, aliases, dry-run settlement and late cutover |
| Provider terms, quotas or schema change | Medium | High | Cached raw responses, terms review, fallback sources and request telemetry |
| Free-source operation consumes substantial manual time | High | Medium | Measure adjudication workload and include it in budget |
| Application cache serves stale ratings after refresh | Medium | Medium | Artifact/input hashes and explicit cache invalidation |
| Scope expands to other national-team categories | Medium | Medium | Registry eligibility and explicit separate project approval |

## 15. Decisions required

The decision-maker must approve or change:

1. **Core scope:** current FIFA senior men's teams, with associates gated by evidence.
2. **MVP:** competitive predictions without automated betting.
3. **Worldwide promise:** conditional on the Phase 1 fixture gate.
4. **Friendly policy:** predictions allowed; betting blocked until prospective evidence passes.
5. **Cutover policy:** production rename occurs last.
6. **Evidence period:** one complete high-volume international window.
7. **Provider budget:** deferred until measured quota and coverage results exist.
8. **Non-FIFA expansion:** explicitly deferred unless separately approved.
9. **Betting authority:** competitions enabled individually, not globally.
10. **Stop authority:** Phase 1 may recommend a narrower product instead of continuing.

## Appendix B — Provider facts to reconfirm in Phase 1

- FIFA's men's calendar from 2026 uses four release periods: March, June, a combined late
  September/early October window, and November, plus final tournaments where applicable.
- The Odds API charges usage by requested markets and regions and supports a selected competition
  catalogue rather than all international football.
- API-Football advertises 100 requests per day on its free plan, with historical-season limits.
- TheSportsDB advertises a free v1 API and a $9/month premium tier with v2 access and higher limits.
- BSD coverage outside the competitions already observed in this repository remains unproven.

Provider pricing, catalogues, terms and limits must be captured with retrieval dates in the Phase 1
evidence document. None of these facts is a procurement commitment.

## Appendix C — Definition of done for “worldwide”

The word **worldwide** may appear in user-facing material only when:

- all six confederations are represented in the observed evidence window;
- the approved canonical team registry defines the population;
- fixture recall and precision meet the published gate;
- unsupported associates and non-FIFA teams are disclosed;
- cancellation, kickoff and neutral-ground quality meet their gates;
- an operational owner and recovery runbook exist.

If those conditions are not met, the product must name the supported competitions or confederations
instead of claiming worldwide coverage.
